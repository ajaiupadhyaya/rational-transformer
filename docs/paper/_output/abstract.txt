In forecasting experiments on AR(1) series, people overreact to news, and more so when the series is less persistent. A rational forecaster who must learn the persistence ρ also departs from the truth at any fixed ρ, because its estimate is pulled towards its prior, so a deviation from the full-information forecast is not evidence of a bias. We train causal transformers from scratch on squared error, on AR(1) sequences with ρ drawn from U[0, 1], at 4 sizes and 5 memory lengths, and compare each with the exact Bayes forecaster that has the same prior and window of data (window-Bayes). Window-Bayes is the squared-error optimum, so the question is what finite training reaches. Hypotheses, margins and decision rules were fixed in a self-registered protocol, made public on 20 November 2026, after the runs. H1, that the largest model with the full window is equivalent to Bayes, is supported: 12 of 12 verdicts, two measures at 6 values of ρ, lie within ±1 percentage point of excess squared error and ±0.02 of implied-persistence gap. H2: with the window cut to 2, 4, 8, 16 observations the largest model is equivalent to window-Bayes at each window, 12 of 12 verdicts each. H3, overreaction where the matched Bayesian underreacts (ρ = 0.6 and 0.8), is not supported: 0 of 8 positive at 95%, none after adjustment; the largest upper end of a 95% interval is +0.0008 against a threshold of +0.02. The verdicts hold at step 30,000, when the learning rate reaches zero; at 10,000 steps the windowed models are not yet equivalent, and the smallest model misses its benchmarks (both exploratory). Descriptively, with the mean and scale unknown a four-observation Bayesian itself overreacts at high persistence, and the human intervals include its curve from ρ = 0.2 upward, so the overlay does not show people overreacting beyond an information-limited Bayesian. One architecture, simulated data.
